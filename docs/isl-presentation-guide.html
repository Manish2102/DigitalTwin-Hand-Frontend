Indian Sign Language: presentation guide

Seven easy signs plus Welcome, chosen to work reliably in a live demo, and how to use them. Seen from the camera, the signer's right hand is blue and the left is orange; a grey hand is resting.

The demo signs: Thank you, Hello, How are you, Good Morning, I, you, Pleased, and Welcome. The first seven come from the ISL recordings the app was trained on. On 28 test videos of signers it had never seen, restricted to these seven: 25 right, 1 wrong (I read as "you"), 2 "not sure". Welcome is not in that dataset, so it works only after you teach it (step 4): it then matches your sign. Choose the sign you use for Welcome (or take it from the ISLRTC dictionary) and sign it the same way every time.

1. Before your presentation (5 minutes)

  1. Set up. Open Sign language in the app, press Start camera and wait until the badge says "Ready" (the first start takes about 5 seconds to load).
  2. Position. Sit or stand 1 to 1.5 m from the camera. Your head, both shoulders and both hands must be in the picture. Face a light (a window or lamp in front of you, not behind you).
  3. Choose the set. Keep Demo signs selected. This limits the app to the signs above, which is far more accurate than all 106 words.
  4. Teach it Welcome, and your key signs. In Teach it your signs, choose Welcome (first in the list), press Record a sample, sign it the way you will on stage, and lower your hands. Do it 3 times, the same way each time. Repeat for Thank you and any other sign you care about. Recordings stay in your browser. The app boosts a sign when what it sees matches your own recordings, and warns you if your three recordings look too different.
  5. Rehearse. Sign each phrase once and check the text. If a sign comes out as "Not sure", tap the right suggestion, then record one more sample of that sign.

2. How to sign so it is detected

DoWhy
Start with hands down, below the dashed rest line on screenA sign begins when a hand rises above the line.
Sign one word, then lower your hands and wait about half a secondLowering the hands is what ends the sign. The word then appears.
Make the sign clearly and a little slowly, in front of your chestFast, small or hidden movements are missed.
Keep both shoulders in the picture; do not cover your hands with each other unless the sign needs itThe app measures where your hands are relative to your body.
Use the Sign reference button or the picture cards below to check the shapeSigns vary by region; teaching your own samples matches your style.
What it cannot do: read facial expressions, understand ISL grammar, or follow continuous signing without a rest between signs. It turns single signs into words, in the order you sign them.

4. The signs

Each picture shows the start, middle and end of the movement, drawn from the most typical recording of a real signer. The line under the picture is measured from the same recording. These pictures are a reminder, not a course: for a precise model of each sign, watch the ISLRTC video dictionary (islrtc.nic.in) or ask a Deaf signer.

Welcometeach it: not in the dataset

Welcome

There is no recording of Welcome in the training data, so the app cannot know it until you teach it. Use the sign you normally use, or the one in the ISLRTC dictionary. Record it 3 times in Teach it your signs, signing it the same way each time: same hand shape, same place in front of your chest, same movement. The app then recognises it when what it sees closely matches your recordings, and says "not sure" otherwise. If you later change how you sign it, press Forget my recordings and record again.

5. If something goes wrong on the day

ProblemWhat to do
Nothing happens when you signCheck the badge says "Ready" and your hands go above the dashed rest line; make sure both shoulders are in view.
"Not sure" with suggestionsTap the correct suggestion, or sign it again a little slower. Record another sample of that sign beforehand.
A different word appearsUse Undo. Record 3 samples of the sign in Teach it your signs.
It is slow or the camera freezesClose other tabs and apps that use the camera, press Stop camera, then Start camera.
The room is dark or very bright behind youLight your face and hands from the front. Backlight makes the hands hard to track.
You need to be sureType or tap the words you need: tap a sign chip in "Signs it knows" to show its picture, and use Speak on the text.

6. About the data

Words come from INCLUDE (Sridhar, Ganesan, Kumar, Khapra; AI4Bharat and IIT Madras), CC BY 4.0, recorded in Chennai. The recogniser was trained on those recordings (landmarks only). Measured accuracy for the whole 106-word vocabulary is about 87% on the first guess for signers it has not seen; it is higher for a short list. Regional differences exist: if your sign differs from the picture, teach it your version. Full reference: docs/isl-reference.md in the project.
